# Known Issue: BUG-007 Music transients poke through the duck level; hard loop seam; gain-smoothing edge artifact

## Metadata

- Bug ID: `BUG-007`
- Status: fixed (partial — see Unchanged behavior / open risk)
- Severity: medium
- Found in: `briefing/app/services/mixing.py`
- Fixed in: (direct fix, lightweight bug-fix workflow)
- Related requirements: `FR-031`
- Related tests: `TEST-019`

## Current behavior

Reported by Jason after listening to a real generated briefing: "loud drums under the words
sometimes as it loops." Investigation (measuring the actual committed clips, not assuming):

1. All 6 committed clips measure a consistent RMS (~-14.3 to -14.4 dBFS) — **not** an inter-clip
   level-inconsistency problem, ruling out the original "normalize across clips" hypothesis.
2. Every clip has a **~15dB crest factor** (peak vs. RMS) — the source material has a much wider
   dynamic range than the flat `duck_db` gain in `STORY_MIX_PROFILES` was implicitly tuned against.
   A transient (e.g. a drum hit) sits ~15dB above the level the duck was calibrated against, so it
   can approach or exceed real Kokoro narration's RMS (-25.9dBFS, measured) even after ducking.
3. `_loop_to_length` used a hard `np.tile` — no crossfade at the repeat boundary, so any story
   whose narration outlasts one ~25-30s clip gets an audible seam every repeat (not genuinely
   "loopable" despite the generation prompts having asked for a loopable ending).
4. (Found during the fix, not the original report): `_moving_average`'s `np.convolve(...,
   mode="same")` implicitly zero-pads at the array boundary — for the gain-reduction curve this
   falsely assumes silence just outside the buffer, pulling gain toward 0 for roughly the first/last
   `window/2` samples (~20ms) of every clip regardless of actual signal content.

## Expected behavior

Transients are tamed relative to each clip's own level (not erasing the quiet passages that already
sounded right), loop repeats have no audible seam, and gain-smoothing does not introduce a spurious
dip at clip boundaries.

## Unchanged behavior

- `STORY_MIX_PROFILES`/`STRUCTURAL_MIX_PROFILES` `duck_db`/`gain_db` values are unchanged in this
  fix — see Open risk below; deepening them further is a judgment call on overall bed prominence
  that needs a listening decision, not a unilateral code change.
- Overall clip RMS is only modestly affected (~0.5-0.9dB across the 6 clips) — the "already sounds
  good" quiet-passage level is preserved.

## Reproduction steps

1. Load any committed music clip; measure RMS and peak — confirm ~14-15dB crest factor.
2. Duck at any `STORY_MIX_PROFILES` weight; observe the ducked transient peak sits close to or
   above real narration RMS (-25.9dBFS).
3. Loop a clip via the old `np.tile`-based `_loop_to_length` under narration longer than one clip;
   observe an audible seam at each repeat.

## Root cause

1. `mixing.py` had no transient-aware processing — a single flat `duck_db` gain cannot account for
   a clip's internal dynamic range.
2. `_loop_to_length` tiled the raw clip with no seam treatment.
3. `_moving_average` relied on `np.convolve`'s implicit zero-padding, incorrect for a gain-curve
   smoothing use case.

## Fix

- New `_compress_transients()`: fast peak-capture envelope (`scipy.ndimage.maximum_filter1d`, ~8ms
  window — an average would dilute a brief transient rather than detect it) + smoothed gain
  reduction above a ceiling 10dB over the clip's own RMS. Verified on all 6 real clips: peak
  reduced 0.5-2.0dB, RMS shifted only 0.5-0.9dB (aggressive settings were tried and rejected — they
  dragged down overall RMS almost as much as peak, since a large fraction of this material sits
  close to its own peak; see the CR for the rejected alternatives).
- New `_make_loopable_unit()`: blends the clip's tail into its own head with a 1s crossfade before
  tiling. Verified: seam sample-to-sample jump (0.0005) is smaller than typical adjacent-sample
  variation elsewhere in the same track (0.0162).
- `_moving_average()`: edge-value padding instead of zero-padding.

## Open risk (not fixed here — needs a listening decision, not unilateral code change)

Quantified during investigation: with the new limiter, a ducked transient at `medium` weight still
sits only ~0.8dB *below* real narration RMS (light: transient ~2.8dB *above* narration RMS).
Fully guaranteeing transients stay safely under voice would need duck_db deepened by roughly
5-7dB across all weights — but that also makes the already-approved "quiet passage" level of the
bed noticeably subtler (the RMS-below-voice margin would grow from ~12dB to ~17-19dB). This is a
mix-balance tradeoff that can only be judged by ear; flagged to Jason rather than decided here. A
complementary option is regenerating the punchiest clips with an explicit low-dynamic-range prompt,
addressing the crest factor at the source rather than only via post-processing.
